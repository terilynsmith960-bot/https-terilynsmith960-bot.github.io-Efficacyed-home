EfficacyEd

Free, on-demand professional development · Grades K–5

Build the confidence to make new teaching strategies stick

EfficacyEd is for elementary teachers and the coaches and administrators who support them. Most PD shows a strategy once and moves on — we close that gap with short video models, guided practice, and printable tools that grow teachers' confidence to use it with students.

At a glance

Who it’s for, the problem it solves, and what success looks like

Who it’s for

K–5 classroom teachers, from early-career to veteran — plus instructional coaches, grade-level leads, and administrators who facilitate team learning.

The problem

Traditional PD is often a single sit-and-get session, disconnected from a teacher’s own classroom. Teachers hear about a strategy but rarely feel ready to use it. EfficacyEd is available whenever teachers need it — planning period, recess duty, or at home.

What success looks like

Teachers leave each module with a new strategy and a stronger belief that they can execute it well — measured by their own growth, not a test.

Strategy modules

Choose a strategy relevant to your grade band

Every module is offered as a captioned video model, a one-page written summary, and a guided practice prompt — so you can start wherever works best for you.

Watch how open-ended questions reveal student thinking so you can adjust in the moment.

Video: Challenge Innovate Grow: Teacher & Learner Centre, via YouTube.

Formative questioning replaces yes/no checks with open prompts (\u201cWhat makes you think that?\u201d) that reveal student thinking in real time, letting a teacher adjust instruction before misunderstanding takes hold.

Rehearse with a partner: one plays teacher, one plays student. Practice turning a closed question into an open one, three times, before trying it with your class.

Watch a teacher rotate flexible groups through stations so she can work closely with one group at a time.

Video: Edutopia, via YouTube.

Grouping by current need — not a fixed label — lets every student work at an appropriate level of challenge while the teacher gives targeted support to one group at a time.

Sketch a 3-group rotation for one upcoming lesson: what will each group do, and how will you know when to regroup?

Watch a kindergarten teacher use quick, predictable attention signals to bring the class back together.

Video: Teaching Exceptional Kinders by Amy Murray, via YouTube.

A short, predictable routine reduces the instructional time lost to transitions and gives young students a clear, low-anxiety signal for what comes next.

Choose one routine to try this week and rehearse the exact wording and hand signal you'll use, out loud, before Monday.

Copyright & fair use

Using outside material the right way

You already know the basics of copyright online — this page focuses on applying those rules inside a classroom or on a classroom website.

The four fair use factors

Purpose and character of the use

Educational, non-commercial use leans toward fair use — but isn't automatic.

Nature of the copyrighted work

Factual works are more likely to support fair use than highly creative ones.

Amount used

Using a small, necessary portion rather than a whole work strengthens a fair-use claim.

Effect on the market

A use that could replace someone's ability to sell their own work weighs against fair use.

Classroom vs. posted online

Material that's safe to display to your own class under the TEACH Act isn't automatically safe to post publicly on a classroom website — public posting reaches a much wider audience and needs its own permission or license.
